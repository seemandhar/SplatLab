\section{Cross-Run Audit: All 16 Manuscripts}
\label{sec:audit}

Table~\ref{tab:audit} catalogues every paper produced by the autonomous
lab during the study window.  One row per run; one run is one
independent dispatch of the \texttt{/research} master orchestrator on a
fresh ledger and a fresh workspace.  The columns track the target
method (when one was specified at run-start), the evaluation dataset
and scene set, the prompt regime, the method name the pipeline
ultimately committed to in its \texttt{paper/main.pdf}, the locally
reproduced baseline, the final headline result on whichever metric the
run measured, the delta vs.\ that baseline, the number of trained
experiment branches recorded in the ledger, the compute spent in GPU
hours, and an audit verdict on the manuscript's honesty.

\paragraph{Audit verdicts.}  We classify each manuscript as one of:
(\textbf{\checkmark}) verified -- a clean positive $\Delta$ above the
measured noise floor, with reviewer round-2 returning weak-accept or
better and the paper-quality-checker reporting zero remaining critical
hallucinations;
($\circ$) honest-marginal -- the run delivered a $\Delta$ within the
noise floor and the manuscript reframes the contribution accordingly
(LPIPS-Pareto, scene-conditional gain, or a systematic-evaluation
framing) rather than over-claiming PSNR;
($\square$) workshop-style -- the contribution is a benchmark, an
editing pipeline, a calibration channel, or a different-metric task,
where PSNR is not the primary axis;
($\times$) failed -- the run terminated without a positive result on
its own self-stated target.  Verdicts are read directly from the
ledger (\texttt{best.classification}, \texttt{revision\_done}
\texttt{remaining\_critical}, and round-2 \texttt{review\_received}
events) -- nothing in this table is rephrased after the fact.

\paragraph{Prompt regime legend.}
\textsf{free} = open-ended creative prompt (``design a novel
3DGS\ldots'');
\textsf{target} = a specific paper / GitHub repository URL was provided
at run-start as the SOTA to beat;
\textsf{scene} = a custom dataset directory was provided (Nebula,
Bubble, etc.) with the request to beat splatfacto on it;
\textsf{workshop} = a workshop-style brief, often without a single
PSNR target.

\paragraph{Metric notation.}
PSNR is reported in dB unless flagged: $\dagger$~3D view-consistency
IoU; $\ddagger$~instruction-success / edit-locality rate;
$\S$~negative log-likelihood (calibration);
$\P$~AUROC@$25$ (auto-annotation gate). ``--'' means the run produced
no headline number against a single baseline (typically benchmark
papers).

\paragraph{What the table shows.}  Of the sixteen manuscripts:
four (\#1, \#3, \#6, \#12, \#13) are clean verified
positives where our generated method beats its locally-reproduced
baseline by at least the measured noise floor on the headline metric,
and round-2 reviewers returned weak-accept or better;
five (\#2, \#4, \#5, \#11, \#16) are honest-marginal --
the run executed cleanly but its own measurements showed the
$\Delta$ landed within the noise floor, and the manuscript
reframes the contribution as a systematic-evaluation, scene-conditional
gain, or LPIPS-Pareto improvement rather than a PSNR win;
six (\#7, \#8, \#9, \#14, \#15) are workshop-style submissions whose
contribution is a benchmark, calibration channel, or editing pipeline,
where PSNR is not the primary axis; and one (\#10) is a frank failure
recorded in the ledger.  The audit column is read mechanically from
the ledger, not edited after the fact.

\paragraph{Observations.}
(i)~Runs that were prompted with a custom dataset (\textsf{scene}
prompts, rows \#12, \#13) have the largest deltas; both deliberately
attack a previously-untrained scene class where vanilla splatfacto is
known to be weak.
(ii)~Runs targeting a specific recent paper (\textsf{target} prompts,
rows \#3, \#4, \#10) split between one verified beat (FreezeGS, \#3,
$+0.43$\,dB on FastGS), one honest-mixed (CSNVF-NRSH, \#4), and one
acknowledged failure (LockEtaLangevin, \#10) -- consistent with the
literature observation that the recent-SOTA paper graveyard leaves
little headroom.
(iii)~Runs with the highest $N$ (\#12 with 47 trained branches, \#6
with 38) tend to commit cleaner manuscripts with stronger ablations,
suggesting branch-density correlates with audit verdict.
