A1. AbsGS — Recovering Fine Details for 3D Gaussian Splatting
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/absgs.py
Paper:      "AbsGS: Recovering Fine Details for 3D Gaussian Splatting"
Authors:    Zongxin Ye, Wenyu Li, Sidun Liu, Peng Qiao, Yong Dou
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10484
Venue:      ACM Multimedia 2024
Complexity: 1/5 (config-only change)

NOVELTY:
  Identifies "gradient collision" as root cause of over-reconstruction in 3DGS.
  When opposing gradients from different views cancel out, large Gaussians in
  problematic regions never get split. Uses absolute-value gradients (absgrad)
  for densification instead of averaged gradients.

CLAIM:
  Recovers fine details missed by vanilla 3DGS densification. Better rendering
  quality with reduced or comparable memory consumption.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  The simplest possible improvement — a single flag (absgrad=True) that gsplat
  already supports. Demonstrates that even config-level changes can yield
  meaningful quality gains. Used as a building block in many other methods.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  Standard 3DGS averages view-space positional gradients across views, causing
  cancellation. AbsGS takes the absolute value before averaging, ensuring large
  gradients from ANY view trigger splitting. No new parameters, no code changes.

A2. BOGausS — Better Optimized Gaussian Splatting
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/bogauss.py
Paper:      "BOGausS: Better Optimized Gaussian Splatting"
Authors:    Stephane Pateux, Matthieu Gendrin, Luce Morin, Theo Ladune, et al.
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01844
Venue:      EUSIPCO 2025
Complexity: 4/5

NOVELTY:
  Systematically rethinks the 3DGS optimization pipeline through principled
  Bayesian analysis. Four contributions:
  1. Confidence estimation via Variational Bayesian Inference for minimal sizes
  2. Unbiased Sparse Adam optimizer with precision-aware updates
  3. Density-preserving splitting (child opacity/size match parent density)
  4. Distortion-based densification with SNR prioritization + effective opacity pruning

CLAIM:
  Models up to 10x lighter than original 3DGS with no quality degradation.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Addresses the ad-hoc nature of vanilla 3DGS optimization — replacing
  heuristic split/clone/prune with principled rate-distortion optimization.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  - Effective opacity pruning: contribution/surface_area threshold (not just opacity)
  - Density-preserving split: adjusts child sizes/opacities to maintain local density
  - P_SNR scoring for split prioritization
  - Densification every 500 iterations (vs 100 in vanilla)
  - Keeps log-space scale parameterization and standard Adam optimizer base

A3. Compact 3DGS — Gradient-Driven Natural Selection
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/compact_gs.py
Paper:      "Gradient-Driven Natural Selection for Compact 3D Gaussian Splatting"
Authors:    Xiaobin Deng, Qiuli Yu, Changyu Diao, Min Li, Duanqing Xu
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16980
Venue:      arXiv 2025
Complexity: 3/5

NOVELTY:
  Models Gaussian survival as natural selection: rendering gradients = fitness,
  regularization gradients = environmental pressure. Fully learnable end-to-end
  pruning without manual intervention or additional learnable parameters.

CLAIM:
  >0.6 dB PSNR gain over 3DGS with only 15% of Gaussians.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Existing pruning methods use fixed thresholds or require manual tuning.
  This method lets the optimization itself decide what survives.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  Three phases:
  1. Standard densification (0-15K)
  2. Natural selection (15K-23K): L_reg = (E[v] - T)^2 with T=-20 creates
     global downward pressure on opacities. Rendering gradients keep useful
     Gaussians alive; reg gradients kill weak ones.
  3. Fine-tuning (23K-30K)
  Uses soft pruning (zero opacity, keep optimizer state) then hard cleanup.
  Finite opacity prior accelerates selection without hurting effectiveness.

A4. DecompGS — Decomposing Densification for Faster Reconstruction
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/decomp_gs.py
Paper:      "Decomposing Densification in Gaussian Splatting for Faster 3D Scene Reconstruction"
Authors:    Binxiao Huang, Zhengwu Liu, Ngai Wong
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20239
Venue:      arXiv 2025
Complexity: 3/5

NOVELTY:
  Key insight: split operations handle GLOBAL spatial spread while clone
  operations govern LOCAL refinement. Proposes Global-to-Local (G2L)
  densification that sequences them appropriately.

CLAIM:
  ~2x training speedup with comparable or improved quality.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Standard 3DGS runs split+clone simultaneously throughout training, which
  is suboptimal. G2L phases them for better convergence.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  1. G2L densification: global phase (split-only for spatial diffusion) →
     local phase (split+clone for detail refinement)
  2. C2F multi-resolution: FFT-energy-guided schedule. T_r = (E_r/E_1)*T_densify
  3. Adaptive opacity pruning: tau = min(alpha_sorted[floor(N*p)], tau_u)
     replaces fixed 0.005 threshold with distribution-adaptive threshold

A5. Micro-Splatting — Isotropy-Informed Covariance Regularization
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/micro_splatting.py
Paper:      "Micro-splatting: Multistage Isotropy-informed Covariance Regularization"
Authors:    Jee Won Lee, Hansol Lim, Sooyeun Yang, Jongseong Brad Choi
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05740
Venue:      arXiv 2025
Complexity: 3/5

NOVELTY:
  Two-stage pipeline: Stage I grows compact near-isotropic Gaussians with
  trace-based covariance regularization; Stage II prunes low-impact splats
  and merges redundant neighbors via spatial+feature thresholds.

CLAIM:
  Reduces splat count and model size by up to 60%, shortens training by 20%,
  while matching or surpassing SOTA PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Highly anisotropic Gaussians act as low-pass filters, blur high-frequency
  detail, and waste storage. Isotropy regularization fixes this.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  Stage I (Growth, 0→T_refine):
  - Covariance trace regularization: sum of eigenvalues capped at trace_cap
  - Isotropy loss: penalizes ratio of max/min eigenvalue
  - L2 photometric loss additive to splatfacto's L1+SSIM
  - Absgrad for gradient-guided splitting
  Stage II (Micro-Refine, T_refine→end):
  - Importance-score pruning (opacity * scale product)
  - Pairwise merging: spatial < 0.01, color < 0.03, scale < 0.01
  - Soft opacity zeroing preserves Adam momentum

A6. Depth-Regularized GS — Monocular Depth Supervision
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/depth_reg.py
Paper:      "Depth-Regularized Optimization for 3D Gaussian Splatting in Few-Shot Images"
Authors:    Jaeyoung Chung, Jeongtaek Oh, Kyoung Mu Lee
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13398
Venue:      CVPR 2024 Workshop (3DMV) — Best Paper Runner-up
Complexity: 2/5

NOVELTY:
  Uses pretrained monocular depth estimators (Depth Anything V2, ZoeDepth,
  MiDAS) as geometric priors to regularize 3DGS, especially in textureless
  and ambiguous regions.

CLAIM:
  Enables high-quality 3DGS from very few input images by preventing
  geometric overfitting.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Vanilla 3DGS has no geometric prior — it can produce arbitrarily wrong
  geometry while still minimizing photometric loss. Depth regularization
  anchors the geometry.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  - Custom DataManager runs monocular depth estimation at startup, caches to disk
  - Pearson correlation depth loss (scale-invariant) or L1 depth loss
  - Optional opacity entropy regularization for floater removal
  - Zero rendering/densification changes — purely additive loss
  - Note: original paper uses L1 depth; implementation also supports Pearson

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/feature_gs.py
Paper:      "Feature 3DGS: Supercharging 3D Gaussian Splatting to Enable Distilled Feature Fields"
Authors:    Shijie Zhou, Haoran Chang, Sicheng Jiang, et al.
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03203
Venue:      CVPR 2024 (Highlight)
Complexity: 4/5

NOVELTY:
  Extends 3DGS to render arbitrary-dimensional semantic features alongside
  RGB. Each Gaussian stores a latent feature vector; an MLP decodes rendered

CLAIM:
  Enables novel-view semantic segmentation, language-guided editing, and
  segment-anything capabilities on top of 3DGS.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Standard 3DGS only renders RGB. This adds semantic understanding for

TECHNICAL DETAIL:

  - MLP decode head: low-dim latent → high-dim CLIP/DINO features
  - Single fused rasterization pass (RGB + features)
  - Cosine feature distillation loss
  - Replicates features during densification (split_gaussians)
  - PCA visualization in viewer
  - Demonstrates ALL 9 extension points of the gs_template

A8. Opti3DGS — Coarse-to-Fine Frequency Modulation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/opti3dgs.py
Paper:      "Optimized 3D Gaussian Splatting using Coarse-to-Fine Image Frequency Modulation"
Authors:    Umar Farooq, Jean-Yves Guillemaut, Adrian Hilton, Marco Volino
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14475
Venue:      ACM SIGGRAPH CVMP 2025
Complexity: 2/5

NOVELTY:
  Blurs ground-truth training images with progressively smaller Gaussian
  filters during training. Forces model to learn coarse structure first,
  then fine detail. Does NOT change renderer or densification.

CLAIM:
  62% fewer Gaussians, 40% less GPU memory, 20% faster training — no
  quality loss. Highly modular, works with most 3DGS variants.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Standard 3DGS creates unnecessary Gaussians early on to fit high-frequency
  detail before the scene structure is established.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  - Mean blur filter with 5-level step-decay schedule
  - Kernel sizes: 15→11→7→5→3→1 (original)
  - Filtering stops after 12,000 iterations
  - Only modifies GT images for loss computation — zero changes to model
  - scaling_lr doubled to 0.01 (vs default 0.005)
  - densify_grad_thresh lowered to 0.0001 (vs default 0.0002)
  - Naturally produces level-of-detail representations as byproduct

A9. LightGaussian — Unbounded 3D Gaussian Compression
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/light_gs.py
Paper:      "LightGaussian: Unbounded 3D Gaussian Compression with 15x Reduction and 200+ FPS"
Authors:    Zhiwen Fan, Kevin Wang, Kairun Wen, et al.
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17245
Venue:      NeurIPS 2024 (Spotlight)
Complexity: 4/5

NOVELTY:
  Multi-stage post-training compression: significance-based pruning →
  SH distillation via pseudo-view knowledge transfer → VecTree quantization.

CLAIM:
  15x compression, rendering speed boost from 144 to 237 FPS.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Vanilla 3DGS models are massive (hundreds of MB). This makes them
  deployable on resource-constrained devices.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  Six training stages:
  1. Normal training (0→30K)
  2. Global significance pruning at step 30K (prune_ratio=0.5)
  3. Co-adaptation recovery, no densification (30K→35K)
  4. SH distillation with pseudo-views (35K→40K): target_sh_degree=2
  5. Vector quantization of SH coefficients (40K): codebook_size=8192
  6. VQ fine-tuning (40K→45K)
  All transitions happen in after_train() to avoid breaking autograd.

A10. StableGS — Floater-Free Framework
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/stablegs.py, stablegs_datamanager.py
Paper:      "StableGS: A Floater-Free Framework for 3D Gaussian Splatting"
Authors:    Luchao Wang, Qian Ren, Kaimin Liao, et al.
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18458
Venue:      arXiv 2025
Complexity: 4/5

NOVELTY:
  Identifies that floaters persist due to opacity gradient vanishing at a
  pseudo-equilibrium. Proposes Dual Opacity: alpha for geometry (strong
  depth losses), alpha' for appearance (color loss). Two separate rendering
  paths decouple geometry optimization from appearance refinement.

CLAIM:
  Eliminates floaters while resolving the blur-artifact trade-off.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Floaters are the #1 visual artifact in 3DGS. Previous methods either
  suppress them at the cost of detail (blur) or don't suppress them enough.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  - Dual opacity: geometric path (strong depth constraints) vs appearance path
  - DUSt3R depth prior loss + global scale optimization
  - Multi-view self-supervised depth consistency loss
  - Custom DataManager: COLMAP pair selection, DUSt3R depth estimation,
    depth warping data preparation, caching system
  - Pair selection based on co-visibility, rotation angle (16-60 deg),
    homography inlier ratio

A11. VoD-3DGS — View-Dependent Opacity
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/vod3dgs.py
Paper:      "VoD-3DGS: View-opacity-Dependent 3D Gaussian Splatting"
Authors:    Mateusz Nowak, Wojciech Jarosz, Peter Chin
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17978
Venue:      arXiv 2025
Complexity: 3/5

NOVELTY:
  Replaces scalar opacity with view-dependent opacity using a learnable
  symmetric 3x3 matrix (SGGX microflake-inspired). Formula:
  alpha_hat = sigmoid(gamma + w^T * S_hat * w)

CLAIM:
  SOTA real-time performance on specular/reflective scenes with minimal
  memory overhead (+6 params per Gaussian) and negligible FPS drop.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Scalar opacity cannot distinguish between scene objects and specular
  reflections. View-dependent opacity lets Gaussians appear/disappear
  based on viewing angle.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  - 6 new parameters per Gaussian: Sxx, Sxy, Sxz, Syy, Syz, Szz
  - Two variants: [L] largest eigenvalue reset (higher quality),
    [S] smallest eigenvalue reset (more compact)
  - View-consistency regularization loss with optional keypoint weighting
  - Quadratic form clamped to [-2, 2] to prevent sigmoid saturation
  - lr(S_hat) = 1/4 * lr(opacity)

A12. RAIN-GS — SfM-Free Initialization
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/rain_gs.py
Paper:      "Relaxing Accurate Initialization Constraint for 3D Gaussian Splatting"
Authors:    Jaewoo Jung, Jisang Han, Honggyu An, et al. (Korea University)
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09413
Venue:      arXiv 2024
Complexity: 3/5

NOVELTY:
  Demonstrates 3DGS trained from random point clouds can match or beat
  SfM-initialized 3DGS, removing the SfM dependency entirely.

CLAIM:
  On-par or better than SfM-initialized 3DGS from just 10 random points.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  SfM is slow, brittle, and fails on many real-world captures. This
  removes the bottleneck entirely.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  Three components addressing "limited Gaussian transportability":
  1. SLV (Sparse-Large-Variance) init: 10 random points with large variance
  2. Progressive low-pass filter: dynamic eps2d schedule for frequency control
  3. ABE-Split (Adaptive Bound-Expanding Split): adds third Gaussian pushed
     outward to expand spatial coverage during first 10K steps
  Training: 40K iterations (10K warmup + 25K densification + 5K optimization)
  Scale division ratio lowered from 1.6 to 1.4

A13. Trick-GS — Balanced Bag of Tricks
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/trick_gs.py
Paper:      "Trick-GS: A Balanced Bag of Tricks for Efficient Gaussian Splatting"
Authors:    Anil Armagan, Albert Saa-Garriga, Bruno Manganelli, et al.
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14534
Venue:      ICASSP 2025
Complexity: 3/5

NOVELTY:
  Systematically evaluates which efficiency tricks interact positively
  vs negatively, finding the optimal balanced configuration. Five tricks:
  1. Learned Gaussian masks (straight-through estimator)
  2. Per-Gaussian per-SH-band masking
  3. Significance score pruning (ray-hit * opacity * volume)
  4. Progressive training (blur + resolution + Gaussian scale schedule)
  5. Post-training FP16 quantization

CLAIM:
  2x faster training, 40x smaller disk size, 2x faster rendering, with
  comparable accuracy. Targets resource-constrained devices.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Individual tricks are known; the contribution is finding which COMBINATIONS
  work together (some interact negatively).

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  - gaussian_mask: per-Gaussian binary mask, lr=0.5
  - sh_masks: per-SH-band masks, lr=0.05
  - Progressive resolution via splatfacto warmup mechanism
  - Significance = product of ray hits, opacity, and volume
  - Straight-through estimator for discrete mask optimization

A14. CLoD-GS — Continuous Level-of-Detail
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/clod_gs.py
Paper:      "CLoD-GS: Continuous Level-of-Detail via 3D Gaussian Splatting"
Authors:    Zhigang Cheng, Mingchao Sun, Yu Liu, et al.
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09997
Venue:      ICLR 2026 (poster)
Complexity: 3/5

NOVELTY:
  Per-Gaussian learnable distance decay factor (sigma_d) for continuous
  LoD from a single model. Eliminates discrete LoD copies and visual
  "popping" artifacts.

CLAIM:
  Smooth quality-scalable rendering across all performance targets from
  one model. ~1.6% memory overhead.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Traditional discrete LoD requires multiple model copies and has visible
  transitions. This learns a continuous LoD in a unified representation.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  - Per-Gaussian sigma_d parameter (learned, ~1.6% overhead)
  - Opacity modulated by: alpha * exp(-d_norm^2 / (2*sigma_d^2))
  - Virtual distance s_v randomly sampled during training
  - Coarse-to-fine training: s_v schedule from far→near
  - At inference: tune s_v for quality-performance trade-off
  - lr(sigma_d) = 1e-2 (much higher than position lr)

A15. 2DGS — 2D Gaussian Splatting
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/twodgs.py
Paper:      "2D Gaussian Splatting for Geometrically Accurate Radiance Fields"
Authors:    Binbin Huang, Zehao Yu, Anpei Chen, Andreas Geiger, Shenghua Gao
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17888
Venue:      SIGGRAPH 2024
Complexity: 3/5

NOVELTY:
  Replaces 3D Gaussian ellipsoids with 2D oriented planar disks. Enables
  intrinsically view-consistent geometry through perspective-correct
  ray-splat intersection.

CLAIM:
  Noise-free and detailed geometry reconstruction while maintaining
  competitive appearance, fast training, and real-time rendering.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  3D Gaussians have ambiguous geometry — they can represent surfaces but
  also volumetric clouds. 2D disks are inherently surface-like.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  - One scale axis driven toward zero → flat disk degeneration
  - Depth distortion loss: concentrates blending weights at single depth
  - Normal consistency loss: aligns per-Gaussian normals with rendered
    depth gradient normals at median intersection point (opacity=0.5)
  - Uses gsplat 2DGS backend for perspective-correct rasterization
  - Extracts surface normals from Gaussian quaternions

A16. Pixel-GS — Pixel-Aware Gradient Density Control
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/pixel_gs.py
Paper:      "Pixel-GS: Density Control with Pixel-aware Gradient for 3D Gaussian Splatting"
Authors:    Zheng Zhang, Wenbo Hu, Yixing Lao, Tong He, Hengshuang Zhao
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15530
Venue:      ECCV 2024
Complexity: 2/5

NOVELTY:
  Weights densification gradients by the number of pixels each Gaussian
  covers per view. Large Gaussians covering many pixels get properly
  weighted, preventing blurring and needle-like artifacts.

CLAIM:
  Proper growth of large Gaussians in sparse areas. Prevents floater
  growth near camera via depth-based gradient scaling.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Standard 3DGS averages gradients across views without considering pixel
  coverage, causing under-densification in sparsely initialized regions.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  1. Pixel-aware gradient: weight gradient contribution by covered pixel count
  2. Scaled gradient field: depth-dependent scaling in NDC space
  3. Scene radius computed from camera centers (not Gaussian positions)
  - No new parameters, only changes to gradient accumulation logic

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/gaussian_shader.py

ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17977
Venue:      CVPR 2024

NOVELTY:
  Adds per-Gaussian shading attributes (diffuse, specular, roughness,

CLAIM:

  - Renders RGB + normal + expected depth in one pass
  - Normal consistency and opacity sparsity losses
  - Residual SH color introduced later in training schedule
  - SH degree 3, requires depth during training

A18. Scaffold-GS — Anchor-Based Neural Gaussians
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/scaffold_gs.py
Paper:      "Scaffold-GS: Structured 3D Gaussians for View-Adaptive Rendering"
Authors:    Tao Lu, Mulin Yu, Linning Xu, et al.
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00109
Venue:      CVPR 2024 (Highlight)
Complexity: 5/5

NOVELTY:
  Complete architectural redesign: anchor points distribute local "neural
  Gaussians" whose attributes are predicted on-the-fly by MLPs conditioned
  on viewing direction and distance.

CLAIM:
  View-adaptive representation that eliminates per-view redundancy.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Vanilla 3DGS stores fixed attributes per Gaussian. Scaffold-GS makes
  attributes view-dependent via neural prediction, reducing redundancy.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  - Anchor-based: stores anchors (voxelized SfM points), not raw Gaussians
  - MLPs decode k neural Gaussians per visible anchor: opacity, color,
    rotation, scale from anchor features + viewing conditions
  - Multi-resolution feature bank for level-of-detail
  - Anchor growing/pruning replaces split/clone densification
  - Volume regularization instead of scale regularization
  - 5 MLPs: feature_bank, opacity, color, quat, scale

A19. DWTGS — Wavelet Frequency Regularization
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/dwtgs.py
Paper:      "DWTGS: Rethinking Frequency Regularization for Sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting"
Authors:    Hung Nguyen, Runfa Li, An Le, Truong Nguyen
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15690
Venue:      VCIP 2025
Complexity: 2/5

NOVELTY:
  Shows Fourier-based frequency regularization provides negligible benefit
  for sparse-view 3DGS. Wavelet-space (Haar DWT) losses yield +0.3-0.4 dB
  PSNR via spatially localized frequency supervision.

CLAIM:
  +0.3-0.4 dB PSNR for sparse-view scenarios. Wavelets > Fourier for 3DGS.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Fourier transforms lose spatial information. Wavelets provide BOTH
  frequency AND spatial localization, critical for sparse-view settings.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  - Multi-level Haar DWT decomposition (N=2 levels)
  - LF supervision: L1 loss on LL subbands at each level (lambda=0.5)
  - HF regularization: self-supervised sparsity on HH subbands via
    novel-view renders (every 10 iters, stopped after 5K iters, lambda=0.1)
  - No new Gaussian parameters, no rendering changes
  - Purely additive loss-side contribution

A20. LP-3DGS — Learning to Prune
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/lp3dgs.py
Paper:      "LP-3DGS: Learning to Prune 3D Gaussian Splatting"
Authors:    Zhaoliang Zhang, Tianchen Song, Yongjae Lee, et al.
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18784
Venue:      NeurIPS 2024
Complexity: 3/5

NOVELTY:
  Learns a differentiable binary mask per Gaussian using Gumbel-Sigmoid
  reparameterization. Automatically discovers the optimal pruning ratio
  per scene — no hand-tuned thresholds or trial-and-error.

CLAIM:
  Eliminates need for manually preset pruning ratios. Fully differentiable
  and compatible with standard 3DGS training pipeline.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  All prior pruning methods require choosing a threshold or ratio. This
  lets the optimization decide.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  Three phases:
  1. Standard 3DGS (0-19500)
  2. Mask learning with Gumbel-Sigmoid, tau=0.5 (19500-20000, 500 iters)
  3. Hard mask applied, fine-tune (20000-30000)
  Importance scores: "radsplat" (alpha*T), "minisplatting" (sum of color
  weights), or "opacity"
  Soft pruning: zero opacity, don't physically remove (preserve optimizer)
  mask_reg_weight=5e-4 for sparsity

A21. CompGS — Vector Quantization Compression
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/compgs.py
Paper:      "CompGS: Smaller and Faster Gaussian Splatting with Vector Quantization"
Authors:    KL Navaneet, Kossar Pourahmadi Meibodi, et al.
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18159
Venue:      ECCV 2024
Complexity: 3/5

NOVELTY:
  Vector-quantizes Gaussian parameters DURING training (not post-hoc)
  using K-means with Straight-Through Estimator (STE) gradients.

CLAIM:
  40-50x storage reduction, 2-3x rendering speedup with minimal quality loss.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Post-hoc compression loses quality. Training-time VQ lets the model
  adapt to the codebook constraints.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  Three phases:
  1. Standard training (0-15K)
  2. Opacity L1 regularization (15K-20K): drives invisible Gaussians to zero
  3. VQ-aware training (20K-30K): quantization with STE gradients
  Four independent codebooks:
  - Color DC: K=4096
  - Color SH: K=4096
  - Covariance scales: K=16384
  - Covariance rotations: K=16384
  Positions and opacity remain unquantized.

A22. CoR-GS — Sparse-View Co-Regularization
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/cor_gs.py
Paper:      "CoR-GS: Sparse-View 3D Gaussian Splatting via Co-Regularization"
Authors:    Jiawei Zhang, Jiahe Li, Xiaohan Yu, et al.
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12110
Venue:      ECCV 2024
Complexity: 5/5

NOVELTY:
  Key insight: two independent 3DGS fields trained on the same sparse views
  produce disagreements that serve as UNSUPERVISED indicators of reconstruction
  errors. No external priors needed.

CLAIM:
  Self-correction via field disagreement, without external depth/normal priors.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Sparse-view 3DGS needs regularization, but external priors (depth, normals)
  add complexity and potential errors. Co-regularization is self-supervised.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  - Dual-field: Field 1 (splatfacto-managed, kept for inference),
    Field 2 (manual rendering via nn.ParameterDict, discarded)
  - Co-pruning: KNN-based non-matching detection (tau=5.0),
    every 5th refine step, starting at step 500
  - Pseudo-view co-regularization: sample novel cameras near training views,
    minimize L1 + D-SSIM between two field renders (lambda_p=1.0)
  - Field 2 has independent densification (grad_thresh=0.0002)
  - Default: 10K iters for LLFF/DTU, 30K for Mip-NeRF360

A23. FSGS — Few-Shot View Synthesis
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/fsgs.py
Paper:      "FSGS: Real-Time Few-shot View Synthesis using Gaussian Splatting"
Authors:    Zehao Zhu, Zhiwen Fan, Yifan Jiang, Zhangyang Wang
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00451
Venue:      ECCV 2024
Complexity: 3/5

NOVELTY:
  Three contributions for few-shot (3-24 views):
  1. Proximity-guided Gaussian Unpooling: densifies sparse regions via
     midpoints between isolated Gaussians and K nearest neighbors
  2. Pseudo-view synthesis: virtual viewpoints between closest training cameras
  3. Depth regularization: Pearson correlation on real + pseudo views

CLAIM:
  Photo-realistic novel view synthesis from 3 training views with 200+ FPS.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Standard 3DGS densification fails when SfM initialization is extremely
  sparse. Unpooling fills the spatial gaps.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  - Custom DataManager: monocular depth preprocessing
  - Proximity unpooling: K=3, threshold=10.0, every 100 iters from step 500
  - Pseudo-view: starts at iter 2000, noise_std=0.01
  - SH degree progressive: 0→4 every 500 iters
  - Opacity reset at {2000, 5000, 7000} to reset_value=0.05
  - 10K total iterations (not 30K)

A24. MSAA-GS — Multi-Sample Anti-Aliasing
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       gs_template/methods/msaa_gs.py, msaa_gs_enhanced.py
Paper:      "Multi-Sample Anti-Aliasing and Constrained Optimization for 3D Gaussian Splatting"
Authors:    Zheng Zhou, Jia-Chen Zhang, Yu-Jie Xiong, Chun-Ming Xia
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10507
Venue:      arXiv 2025
Complexity: 3/5

NOVELTY:
  Three components:
  1. MSAA: 4x subpixel multi-sample anti-aliasing during rasterization
  2. AWS: Adaptive Weighting Strategy — error-adaptive per-pixel L1 loss
  3. GDC: Gradient Difference Constraints for edge/boundary fidelity

CLAIM:
  Improved SSIM and LPIPS while maintaining real-time rendering.

WHY IT EXISTS:
  Standard 3DGS has aliasing artifacts, especially at object boundaries
  and in fine-detail regions.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  - MSAA: renders same camera at N subpixel offsets (shifted K matrices),
    averages results. Camera optimizer applied once, base params gathered once.
  - AWS: per-pixel weight = alpha * |error|^alpha (focuses on hard pixels)
  - GDC: L1(grad(render) - grad(GT)) for edge fidelity
  - Enhanced variant (msaa_gs_enhanced.py) stacks 6 additional improvements:
    depth reg, bilateral grid, opacity entropy, scale reg, antialiased mode,
    60K training. Expected +2-3 dB over vanilla.

A25. FDS-GS — Frequency-Aware Density Control
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
File:       (base paper, AAAI 2025)
Paper:      "Frequency-Aware Density Control via Reparameterization for High-Quality Rendering of 3D Gaussian Splatting"
Authors:    Zhaojie Zeng, Yuesong Wang, Lili Ju, Tao Guan
ArXiv:      https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07000
Venue:      AAAI 2025
Complexity: 3/5

NOVELTY:
  Establishes direct relation between density and scale through
  reparameterization of scaling parameters, ensuring density responds
  to frequency content.

CLAIM:
  Better quality with fewer Gaussians (SSIM 0.835, PSNR 27.95 on MipNeRF360).

WHY IT EXISTS:
  3DGS lacks an explicit constraint linking density and scale. High-frequency
  regions should have dense small Gaussians; low-frequency regions should
  have sparse large ones.

TECHNICAL DETAIL:
  1. Density-scale coupling: decomposes scale into absolute scalar + relative
     scale vector, linking density to frequency content
  2. Dynamic percentile threshold: sorts gradients, adaptively adjusts
     densification threshold based on distribution (not fixed)
  3. Scale-based filter: deletes Gaussians with improper scale

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B1. Innovation Taxonomy
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every method in this catalog falls into one or more of these innovation categories:

DENSIFICATION INNOVATIONS (how/when to add Gaussians):
  - AbsGS: absolute gradient criterion
  - Pixel-GS: pixel-count-weighted gradients
  - RAIN-GS: ABE-Split for bound expansion from random init
  - DecompGS: G2L (global split-only → local split+clone)
  - FSGS: proximity-guided unpooling for sparse regions
  - FDS-GS: dynamic percentile threshold
  - Scaffold-GS: anchor growing/pruning

PRUNING/COMPRESSION INNOVATIONS (how to reduce Gaussians):
  - Compact 3DGS: natural-selection gradient competition
  - LP-3DGS: learnable Gumbel-Sigmoid masks
  - LightGaussian: significance pruning + SH distillation + VQ
  - CompGS: training-time vector quantization with STE
  - Trick-GS: learned masks + significance scoring + SH band masking
  - Micro-Splatting: importance-score pruning + pairwise merging
  - Opti3DGS: fewer Gaussians via coarse-to-fine GT blurring
  - BOGausS: effective opacity pruning (contribution/area)

LOSS/SUPERVISION INNOVATIONS (what to optimize):
  - Depth-Reg GS: monocular depth supervision
  - DWTGS: wavelet frequency regularization
  - MSAA-GS: adaptive weighting + gradient difference
  - 2DGS: depth distortion + normal consistency
  - CoR-GS: pseudo-view co-regularization
  - FDS-GS: DWT + gradient-diff + density-scale coupling

REPRESENTATION INNOVATIONS (what a Gaussian stores/means):
  - 2DGS: 2D planar disks instead of 3D ellipsoids
  - Scaffold-GS: anchor + neural Gaussian architecture
  - VoD-3DGS: view-dependent opacity matrix
  - GaussianShader: diffuse/specular/roughness/normals + cubemap
  - Feature 3DGS: per-Gaussian semantic features + MLP decoder
  - CLoD-GS: per-Gaussian distance decay for continuous LoD

INITIALIZATION INNOVATIONS (where Gaussians start):
  - RAIN-GS: random/sparse initialization (no SfM needed)
  - FSGS: proximity-guided initial placement

DOMAIN-SPECIFIC INNOVATIONS (specialized physics/rendering):
  - GaussianShader: reflective surfaces
  - StableGS: floater elimination

TRAINING SCHEDULE INNOVATIONS (when/how optimization changes):
  - Opti3DGS: coarse-to-fine GT frequency schedule
  - DecompGS: FFT-energy-guided resolution schedule

B2. Composability Map — How Published Methods Can Be Combined
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Key composable technique families (methods whose innovations stack well):

DENSITY + LOSS:     FDS-GS(density-scale) + DWTGS(wavelet) + MSAA-GS(grad-diff)
DEPTH + GEOMETRY:   Depth-Reg(depth) + 2DGS(normals) + Pixel-GS(pixel-aware grad)
COMPRESSION:        Compact-3DGS(pruning) + CompGS(VQ) + LightGaussian(distillation)
SPARSE-VIEW:        CoR-GS(dual-field) + FSGS(unpooling) + DWTGS(freq-reg)
QUALITY:            AbsGS(absgrad) + MSAA-GS(anti-alias) + Micro-Splatting(cov-reg)
REPRESENTATION:     Scaffold-GS(anchors) + VoD-3DGS(view-dep) + GaussianShader(shading)

KEY PATTERN: The most successful combinations use 2-3 published techniques
with 1-2 genuinely novel ideas. Pure combinations without novel elements
tend to underperform original papers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B3. Open Research Gaps (Opportunities for the Ideator Agent)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Based on analyzing all 25 methods, these gaps remain unaddressed:

GAP 1: ADAPTIVE DENSIFICATION + LOSS CO-DESIGN
  No method jointly optimizes WHEN to densify and WHAT loss to use.
  FDS-GS comes closest but treats them independently.
  Opportunity: loss-aware densification (densify where loss is highest,
  using loss gradients to guide split decisions).

GAP 2: CROSS-SCENE TRANSFER LEARNING
  Every method trains from scratch. No method transfers knowledge
  (optimal hyperparameters, density patterns, loss schedules) across scenes.
  Opportunity: meta-learning for 3DGS hyperparameters.

GAP 3: SELF-SUPERVISED QUALITY ESTIMATION
  CoR-GS uses dual-field disagreement, but no method has a general-purpose
  per-pixel quality estimator that guides optimization.
  Opportunity: learned quality predictor that replaces GT during training.

GAP 4: UNIFIED COMPRESSION-QUALITY FRAMEWORK
  Compact-3DGS, LP-3DGS, CompGS, LightGaussian each solve compression
  differently. No method provides a single knob for quality-size trade-off.
  Opportunity: combine CLoD-GS's continuous LoD with CompGS's VQ for
  continuous compression.

GAP 5: PHYSICS-INFORMED GENERAL SCENES
  GaussianShader handles reflective surfaces but no method applies
  physics-informed rendering to general scenes (lighting, shadows,
  material estimation).
  Opportunity: extend GaussianShader with learned physics priors.

GAP 6: DYNAMIC SCENES
  All 25 methods target static scenes. None addresses temporal consistency,
  motion estimation, or dynamic Gaussian management.
  Opportunity: extend any of these to 4DGS/dynamic settings.

GAP 7: ROBUST DEPTH SUPERVISION
  No method handles fundamentally wrong monocular depth (systematic bias,
  scale drift). Confidence-weighting depth priors is an open problem.
  Opportunity: self-calibrating depth that detects and corrects estimator failures.

GAP 8: FREQUENCY-DOMAIN DENSIFICATION
  DWTGS uses wavelets for LOSSES, but no method uses frequency analysis
  to guide WHERE to densify.
  Opportunity: densify in image regions with high-frequency residuals.

GAP 9: EFFICIENT MULTI-VIEW CONSISTENCY
  Full multi-view consistency (like NeRF's volumetric rendering) remains
  unexplored for 3DGS. Sampling hard views for consistency is promising.
  Opportunity: lightweight multi-view consistency without rendering all views.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B4. Lessons for the Ideator Agent
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT WORKS:
1. COMBINE 2-3 published ideas with 1-2 novel elements (not more)
2. Additive losses are safest — they don't break existing training
3. Scheduling matters: curriculum, warmup, phase transitions > static weights
4. Share computation across techniques (e.g. reuse DWT cache for pruning)
5. Soft pruning preserves optimizer state — always preferred over hard deletion
6. Confidence-weighting external priors >> blind trust
7. Config-level changes (AbsGS: absgrad=True) can be surprisingly powerful
8. The "gentle nudge" regime (lambda < 0.01) works better than strong priors
9. Linear warmup prevents early-training instability from new losses
10. Per-Gaussian parameters are cheap (~1.6% overhead per scalar)
11. Methods that only add losses (no rendering changes) are most composable
12. Custom DataManagers unlock domain-specific preprocessing
13. The best methods address a specific FAILURE MODE, not general "improvement"

ANTI-PATTERNS (what doesn't work):
14. Too many losses at once without scheduling → training instability
15. Strong depth supervision throughout training → blurs fine detail
16. Hard pruning during active densification → thrashes optimizer
17. Post-hoc compression (vs training-time VQ) → quality loss
18. Fixed thresholds across scenes → suboptimal per-scene behavior
19. Ignoring optimizer state during parameter surgery → convergence issues
